Activity Summary

How Many Calories Does Walking Burn?

Walking is a common form of physical activity that can contribute to daily energy expenditure. The number of calories burned depends on several factors, including body weight, walking speed, duration, terrain and individual physiology.

A person who weighs more may use more energy to cover the same distance than a lighter person at a similar pace. Walking faster or walking on an incline can also increase the energy required.

This calculator provides an estimate using metabolic equivalent (MET) values. It is designed for general fitness awareness, not precise measurement of an individual's calorie expenditure.

How to Use the Walking Calories Calculator

Walking Calories Formula

The calculator uses a standard MET-based estimation approach. MET stands for metabolic equivalent of task and represents the energy cost of an activity relative to resting.

Gross Calories = MET × 3.5 × Weight (kg) ÷ 200 × Duration (minutes)

For net activity calories, an estimated resting MET of 1 is subtracted from the activity MET:

Net Calories = (MET − 1) × 3.5 × Weight (kg) ÷ 200 × Duration (minutes)

These equations provide approximate energy expenditure. Real-world calorie use can differ from formula estimates.

Example: Calories Burned Walking for 30 Minutes

Suppose a person weighs 70 kg and walks at a moderate pace for 30 minutes. Using an illustrative MET value of 3.5:

Gross calories = 3.5 × 3.5 × 70 ÷ 200 × 30
Estimated gross calories = 128.6 kcal

The estimated net activity calories are lower because they subtract the energy that would have been used at rest during the same period.

Factors That Affect Calories Burned While Walking

1. Body Weight

Body weight is an important input in MET-based calorie estimates. At a similar activity intensity and duration, a higher body weight generally results in a higher estimated energy expenditure.

2. Walking Speed

Walking speed affects activity intensity. A faster pace generally requires more energy per minute, although total calories also depend on how long you walk.

3. Walking Duration

Longer walks generally use more total energy than shorter walks at the same intensity.

4. Terrain and Incline

Hills, slopes, sand and uneven surfaces can increase physical effort. The terrain adjustment in this calculator is only a broad estimate, not a measurement of the actual incline.

5. Individual Differences

Fitness, walking efficiency, age, environment and other individual factors can affect actual energy expenditure.

Walking for General Fitness

  • Choose a comfortable pace that matches your current fitness level.
  • Increase walking time and distance gradually.
  • Wear comfortable footwear suitable for your route.
  • Use safe, well-lit routes and stay aware of traffic and surroundings.
  • Include rest and recovery when needed.
  • Track consistency and how you feel, not only calorie estimates.
Important: Calorie estimates are not exact measurements and should not be used as a precise food allowance or as the sole basis for weight-management decisions. If you have a medical condition or have been advised to limit physical activity, follow your healthcare professional's guidance.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. How many calories does walking burn in 30 minutes?

It depends on your weight and walking intensity. A 70 kg person walking at a moderate pace may burn roughly 129 gross kcal in 30 minutes using the illustrative MET estimate in this calculator.

2. How many calories does walking burn in one hour?

One-hour calorie expenditure depends on body weight, speed and terrain. Enter your details to get an individualized estimate based on the calculator's assumptions.

3. Does walking faster burn more calories?

Faster walking generally increases energy use per minute. However, total calories also depend on how long you walk and the distance covered.

4. Does body weight affect walking calories?

Yes. Body weight is included in the MET-based formula. At the same estimated activity intensity and duration, a higher body weight generally produces a higher calorie estimate.

5. What is the difference between gross and net calories?

Gross calories include estimated resting energy during the activity period. Net activity calories subtract an estimated resting component to show energy above rest.

6. Is walking on a hill different from walking on a flat surface?

Hilly routes can require more energy than flat routes. The calculator applies a simple illustrative adjustment, but actual energy use depends on the slope and terrain.

7. Are walking calorie calculator results accurate?

They are estimates based on activity assumptions. Individual energy expenditure can differ due to fitness, walking technique, terrain and other factors.

8. Can I use this calculator for treadmill walking?

Yes, as a broad estimate. Choose the speed category closest to your treadmill pace. Incline and treadmill conditions may change actual energy expenditure.
