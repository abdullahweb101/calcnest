What Is Cost Per Acquisition (CPA)?

Cost Per Acquisition, commonly shortened to CPA, is a digital marketing metric that measures how much a business spends to generate one specified action. Depending on the campaign, that action could be a completed purchase, qualified lead, app installation, registration, subscription or another conversion.

Businesses use CPA to evaluate paid advertising campaigns, compare marketing channels, plan campaign budgets and understand the cost associated with generating new customers or leads.

Example:

Suppose an advertising campaign costs $1,000 and generates 50 completed acquisitions. Its average CPA is $20. This means the campaign spent an average of $20 for each recorded acquisition.

CPA Calculator Formula

The standard formula for calculating cost per acquisition is:

CPA = Total Campaign Cost ÷ Number of Acquisitions

For example:

CPA = $1,000 ÷ 50 = $20

The result is an average. Individual conversions may have different costs, but the formula divides the total measured campaign cost by the total number of acquisitions.

Calculate a Campaign Budget

If you know your target CPA and the number of acquisitions you want, you can estimate the required campaign budget.

Required Budget = Target CPA × Target Acquisitions

Example: If your target CPA is $20 and you want 100 acquisitions, the estimated budget is $2,000.

Estimate Acquisitions From a Budget

Estimated Acquisitions = Available Budget ÷ Expected CPA

Example: A $2,000 budget at an expected CPA of $25 could generate approximately 80 acquisitions if the expected average cost is achieved.

CPA vs CPC vs CPM vs ROAS

These metrics measure different parts of advertising performance. They should not be treated as interchangeable.

What Affects Your Cost Per Acquisition?

1. Audience Targeting

Campaigns shown to audiences with little interest in your offer may generate fewer conversions. Relevant targeting can improve the likelihood that clicks turn into meaningful actions.

2. Landing Page Experience

A slow, confusing or poorly designed landing page can cause visitors to leave without completing the intended action. Clear information, mobile usability and a straightforward conversion process are important.

3. Advertising Creative

Images, videos, headlines and calls to action influence whether people engage with an advertisement and continue to the conversion stage.

4. Offer and Pricing

Your product, pricing, discounts, shipping terms and overall value proposition can influence how many visitors become customers.

5. Conversion Tracking

Incorrect tracking, duplicate events or inconsistent attribution windows can distort CPA calculations. Make sure your conversion definition and reporting method are consistent.

6. Competition and Advertising Costs

Competition, audience demand, placement, seasonality and platform bidding conditions can change the cost of reaching potential customers.

CPA and Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)

CPA and Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) are related, but their definitions can differ between businesses.

CPA is often used for a specific campaign and a defined action, such as a lead or purchase. CAC usually represents the broader cost of acquiring a new customer and may include advertising, sales salaries, agency fees, software and other acquisition-related costs.

For a complete business analysis, make sure you know which costs are included in each metric and whether the conversion represents a new paying customer or another action.

How to Evaluate Whether Your CPA Is Sustainable

A low CPA does not automatically mean a campaign is profitable. Compare acquisition costs with the value and quality of the customers being acquired.

  • Compare CPA with the gross profit generated by a sale.
  • Consider refunds, cancellations and customer retention.
  • Include relevant sales and marketing expenses where appropriate.
  • Compare customer lifetime value with acquisition cost.
  • Check whether leads become qualified prospects or paying customers.
  • Use consistent attribution methods when comparing campaigns.
Business reminder

A campaign with a higher CPA can sometimes be more valuable if it attracts customers who spend more, stay longer or generate greater profit. Evaluate acquisition cost alongside customer quality and business economics.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What does CPA stand for?

CPA stands for Cost Per Acquisition or Cost Per Action. It measures the average cost of generating a defined conversion through a marketing campaign.

2. How do I calculate CPA manually?

Divide the total campaign cost by the number of completed acquisitions. For example, $600 divided by 30 acquisitions gives a CPA of $20.

3. What is a good CPA for advertising?

There is no universal good CPA. It depends on your margins, product price, customer lifetime value, business expenses and the quality of the conversions.

4. Is CPA the same as customer acquisition cost?

Not always. CPA often measures a specific campaign action, while CAC generally includes the broader costs of acquiring a new customer.

5. What is the difference between CPA and CPC?

CPC measures the cost of an advertising click. CPA measures the cost of a completed acquisition or other defined conversion.

6. Can I calculate a required advertising budget?

Yes. Multiply your target CPA by the number of acquisitions you want. This provides a simple estimated budget, assuming the target CPA is achieved.

7. Can CPA be higher than the price of a product?

Yes. CPA can exceed the selling price. Whether this is sustainable depends on margins, repeat purchases, customer lifetime value and other revenue sources.

8. Does CPA include agency and creative costs?

Only if those costs are included in the campaign cost you enter. You can include relevant expenses to calculate a broader campaign-level CPA.

9. How can I lower my CPA?

Test more relevant audiences, improve landing pages, refine advertising creative, review your offer and improve conversion tracking. Measure the quality of conversions as well as their cost.

10. Why is my CPA different across advertising platforms?

Platforms may use different attribution windows, conversion definitions, reporting methods and campaign costs. Compare results only after checking that the measurement methods are reasonably consistent.
