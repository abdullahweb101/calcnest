What Is Body Fat Percentage?

Body fat percentage represents the proportion of your total body weight that consists of fat mass. For example, a person weighing 80 kg with an estimated body fat percentage of 20% would have approximately 16 kg of fat mass.

The remaining body weight is commonly described as fat-free mass or lean body mass. It includes muscle, bones, organs, water, and other non-fat tissues.

Important: Body fat percentage is only one measure of body composition. A single estimate cannot determine overall health, fitness, or medical risk.

How Does the US Navy Body Fat Method Work?

The US Navy circumference method estimates body fat using height and selected body circumference measurements. It uses different equations for men and women.

Male Equation

Body Fat % = 86.010 × log10(waist − neck) − 70.041 × log10(height) + 36.76

Female Equation

Body Fat % = 163.205 × log10(waist + hip − neck) − 97.684 × log10(height) − 78.387

The formulas above use inches for all measurements. The calculator automatically converts metric measurements into inches before applying the equations.

Estimated Fat Mass

Fat Mass = Body Weight × (Body Fat Percentage ÷ 100)

Estimated Lean Body Mass

Lean Body Mass = Total Body Weight − Fat Mass

How to Measure Your Body Correctly

Neck Measurement

Place the measuring tape around the neck below the larynx. Keep the tape level and avoid compressing the skin.

Waist Measurement

Follow the measurement location used by the chosen method. For the Navy circumference approach, the male measurement is taken around the abdomen. Keep the tape horizontal and measure without pulling it tightly.

Hip Measurement

For the female equation, measure around the widest part of the hips and buttocks. Keep the tape level.

Height and Weight

Measure height without shoes and use a reliable scale for body weight. Enter measurements in the units selected in the calculator.

Example Body Fat Calculation

Consider a hypothetical male adult with these measurements:

  • Height: 175 cm (68.9 inches)
  • Waist: 85 cm (33.5 inches)
  • Neck: 38 cm (15 inches)
  • Weight: 75 kg

The male Navy equation uses the difference between waist and neck circumference, together with height, to estimate body fat percentage.

If the resulting estimate were 20%, estimated fat mass would be 75 × 0.20 = 15 kg. Estimated lean body mass would be 75 − 15 = 60 kg.

The 20% figure is an illustration of the mass calculations, not a claimed result for every person with these measurements.

What Can Affect Body Fat Estimates?

Measurement Technique

Small differences in tape position, posture, and tension can change circumference readings and therefore the result.

Body Shape

People with different body shapes may not fit the assumptions behind a circumference-based equation equally well.

Hydration and Weight Changes

Changes in hydration can affect body weight. Since fat mass and lean mass are calculated using body weight, the estimates can change as well.

Individual Differences

Age, muscle distribution, health, and other personal characteristics can influence how well a general equation represents an individual.

Body Fat Calculator FAQs

1. What is body fat percentage?

It is the proportion of total body weight made up of fat mass, expressed as a percentage.

2. How accurate is the US Navy method?

It provides an estimate based on circumference measurements. Accuracy varies with measurement technique and individual body shape. It is not a direct measurement of body fat.

3. Why does the calculator ask for neck measurement?

Neck circumference is one of the inputs in the Navy equations and helps produce the estimated result.

4. Why do women need a hip measurement?

The female version of the equation uses waist, hip, neck, and height measurements.

5. What is lean body mass?

Lean body mass is estimated total body weight minus estimated fat mass. It includes muscle, bone, organs, water, and other non-fat tissues.

6. Is body fat percentage the same as BMI?

No. BMI compares weight with height. Body fat percentage estimates the proportion of weight made up of fat.

7. Can I use this calculator to track progress?

You can use it to observe changes in estimates over time. For more consistent comparisons, measure at similar times and use the same measurement technique.

8. Is this calculator suitable for everyone?

It is designed as a general adult educational tool. It may not be suitable for children, teenagers, pregnancy, or people whose body measurements fall outside the assumptions of the equations.
