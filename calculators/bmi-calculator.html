What Is Body Mass Index (BMI)?

Body Mass Index, commonly called BMI, is a numerical value calculated from a person's weight and height. It is widely used as a simple screening measure to classify weight ranges in adults.

A BMI calculation can help provide an initial reference point. However, it does not directly measure body fat, muscle mass, bone density or fat distribution. Two people with the same BMI can have different body compositions and health profiles.

This CalcNest BMI calculator is designed for adults aged 20 and older. It supports both metric and imperial measurements and provides an estimate based on the information entered.

How to Calculate BMI

BMI is calculated by dividing body weight by height squared. The formula depends on the measurement system you use.

Metric BMI Formula

BMI = Weight (kg) ÷ [Height (m) × Height (m)]

For example, if an adult weighs 70 kg and is 1.75 metres tall:

BMI = 70 ÷ (1.75 × 1.75) = 22.86

Imperial BMI Formula

BMI = 703 × Weight (lb) ÷ [Height (in) × Height (in)]

For imperial measurements, enter weight in pounds and height in total inches. The calculator converts the measurements through the standard imperial BMI formula.

Why BMI Has Limitations

BMI is convenient, but it cannot provide a complete picture of an individual's health. It uses only height and weight, so it does not distinguish between fat mass and muscle mass.

Muscle Mass and Athletic Builds

People with substantial muscle mass may have a higher BMI even when they have relatively low body fat. BMI alone cannot distinguish muscle from fat.

Age and Body Composition

Body composition changes with age. BMI may not describe the same health risks equally well across all age groups.

Pregnancy and Children

Adult BMI categories should not be used to assess children or teenagers. Their BMI is interpreted using age- and sex-specific growth charts. Pregnancy also requires a different clinical context.

Other Health Measurements

Depending on the individual, a healthcare professional may consider waist circumference, blood pressure, medical history, laboratory results, physical activity and other measurements alongside BMI.

Frequently Asked Questions About BMI

1. What is BMI?

BMI stands for Body Mass Index. It is a screening measurement calculated using weight and height to classify adult weight ranges.

2. What is a healthy BMI for adults?

A BMI from 18.5 to 24.9 is commonly classified as the healthy weight range for adults. This is a general screening reference, not a guarantee of health.

3. Can BMI tell me my body fat percentage?

No. BMI does not directly calculate body fat percentage. It uses only weight and height.

4. Is BMI different for men and women?

The standard adult BMI calculation formula is the same for men and women. However, BMI does not account for every difference in body composition.

5. Can I use this calculator for children?

No. This tool uses adult BMI categories. Children and teenagers need age- and sex-specific BMI percentile assessments.

6. Does a normal BMI mean I am completely healthy?

No. BMI is only one screening measure. It cannot assess every health condition or replace a medical evaluation.

7. Why does BMI use height squared?

The BMI formula divides weight by height squared to create a standardized index that relates body weight to height.

Yes. The CalcNest BMI calculator is free to use and works directly in your browser without creating an account.
