How to Calculate Gravel Volume

Gravel volume is the amount of space that needs to be filled with aggregate. To estimate it, calculate the surface area and multiply that area by the required depth.

Keep your length, width and depth measurements in compatible units. The calculator converts the measurements into meters internally, so you can use feet or meters for the area and inches, centimeters, feet or meters for depth.

Rectangular Area = Length × Width
Circular Area = π × (Diameter ÷ 2)²
Triangular Area = (Base × Height) ÷ 2
Base Gravel Volume = Area × Gravel Depth
Volume with Extra = Base Volume × (1 + Extra Percentage)
Weight = Volume × Bulk Density

The volume calculation gives a geometric estimate. It does not automatically account for the reduction in loose material volume caused by compaction. The extra allowance is a separate planning adjustment that you can select.

A cubic yard is a common ordering unit in some markets, while cubic meters and tonnes are commonly used in others.

How Much Does Gravel Weigh?

Gravel weight depends on the type of stone, particle size distribution, void spaces, moisture and compaction. A loose cubic meter of aggregate can have a different weight from a compacted cubic meter.

For a rough planning estimate, this calculator offers bulk density choices between 1.4 and 1.7 tonnes per cubic meter. These are illustrative values, not guaranteed supplier specifications.

Estimated Weight (tonnes) = Gravel Volume (m³) × Bulk Density (tonnes/m³)

If you are ordering by weight, ask the quarry or supplier for the material's stated bulk density or typical tonnes-per-cubic-meter conversion.

Choosing the Right Gravel Depth

The appropriate depth depends on the intended use, ground conditions, aggregate grading and the construction design. A decorative garden layer may need a different depth from a vehicle driveway or a load-bearing sub-base.

  • Garden beds: Choose a depth suited to the decorative material and intended coverage.
  • Footpaths: Consider the ground preparation, aggregate size and expected foot traffic.
  • Driveways: Account for vehicle loads, drainage, soil condition and suitable base layers.
  • Drainage applications: Use a specified aggregate grading and design rather than relying on volume alone.

Do not use a generic depth estimate for structural or drainage work. Follow the project specification or seek advice from a qualified contractor.

Understanding Gravel Waste and Compaction

A gravel project may need more material than the simple geometric volume suggests. Uneven ground, loose edges, handling losses and compaction can affect the amount ordered.

The extra percentage in this calculator is an adjustable purchasing allowance. It is not a scientifically determined compaction factor. If the project specification gives a required compacted depth or conversion factor, use that information when planning the order.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. How much gravel do I need for a driveway?

Measure the driveway length and width, select the required depth and add an appropriate extra allowance. For driveways, also consider the ground preparation and the specified aggregate layers.

2. How many cubic yards are in a cubic meter?

One cubic meter is approximately 1.30795 cubic yards. One cubic yard is exactly 27 cubic feet.

3. Can I calculate gravel for a circular area?

Yes. Select Circle and enter the diameter. The calculator uses the circle area formula and multiplies it by the selected depth.

4. Can I calculate gravel for a triangular area?

Yes. Select Triangle and enter its base and perpendicular height. The area is calculated as half the base multiplied by the height.

5. How much does one cubic yard of gravel weigh?

It varies with the aggregate and its moisture content. Use the supplier's density information for a reliable weight estimate.

6. Does the calculator include extra material?

Yes. You can select an extra allowance from 0% to 20%. This increases the calculated volume before weight and cost estimates.

7. Can I estimate gravel bags?

Yes. Choose a bag size in kilograms. The tool divides the estimated material weight by the selected bag weight and rounds up to a whole bag.

8. Is gravel volume the same as compacted volume?

No. Loose gravel can settle and compact. The calculator estimates geometric volume and does not independently model compaction.

9. Does the cost estimate include delivery?

No. The cost estimate covers the selected material pricing basis only. Delivery, loading, taxes and installation are not included.

10. Which gravel density should I choose?

Choose the closest available estimate only if you do not have supplier data. For purchasing by weight, ask the supplier for the actual bulk density of the selected material.
