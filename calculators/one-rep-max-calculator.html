What Is a One Rep Max (1RM)?

A one-repetition maximum, commonly written as 1RM, is the heaviest weight a person can lift for one complete repetition of a particular exercise while maintaining appropriate technique. It is often used in strength training to describe performance and plan resistance-training intensity.

Your 1RM can be estimated from a submaximal set. For example, if you lift 80 kg for five repetitions, a formula can estimate the weight you might be able to lift once. This avoids the need to test a true maximum every time you want to plan your training.

One Rep Max Formulas

Different formulas estimate maximum strength in different ways. Their results can vary, especially at higher repetition counts. This calculator includes five commonly referenced estimation equations.

1. Epley Formula

1RM = Weight × (1 + Repetitions ÷ 30)

The Epley formula is a widely used estimation method for resistance training. It is often applied to sets with moderate repetition counts.

2. Brzycki Formula

1RM = Weight × 36 ÷ (37 − Repetitions)

The Brzycki equation is another popular method. Its estimate becomes less suitable as repetitions approach its upper mathematical limit.

3. Lander Formula

1RM = (100 × Weight) ÷ (101.3 − 2.67123 × Repetitions)

The Lander equation provides an alternative estimate based on weight and completed repetitions.

4. Lombardi Formula

1RM = Weight × Repetitions0.10

The Lombardi formula uses a power relationship between the repetitions and the weight lifted.

5. O'Conner Formula

1RM = Weight × (1 + 0.025 × Repetitions)

This equation is a straightforward alternative for estimating a maximum from a completed set.

Example: Estimating a Bench Press 1RM

Suppose someone completes five bench press repetitions using 80 kg. Using the Epley formula:

1RM = 80 × (1 + 5 ÷ 30)
1RM = 80 × 1.1667
Estimated 1RM ≈ 93.33 kg

This is a mathematical estimate, not a guarantee that the person can safely lift exactly 93.33 kg.

Why Can 1RM Estimates Differ?

  • Exercise type: Performance varies between bench press, squat, deadlift and other movements.
  • Technique: Range of motion and lifting form can affect repetitions and estimated strength.
  • Training experience: Experienced lifters may perform differently from beginners at the same repetition count.
  • Fatigue: Sleep, recovery, nutrition and recent training can influence performance.
  • Formula selection: Each equation uses a different mathematical relationship.
Safety note: Estimated 1RM values are not medical or coaching advice. Do not attempt a true maximum lift without appropriate experience, equipment, supervision and safety precautions. Stop if you experience pain, dizziness or unusual symptoms. Use conservative loads and proper technique.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What does 1RM mean in weightlifting?

1RM means one-repetition maximum. It is the heaviest load a person can lift for one complete repetition of a specific exercise with appropriate technique.

2. Is an estimated one rep max accurate?

It is an approximation rather than an exact measurement. The estimate depends on the exercise, repetition count, technique, fatigue and formula used.

3. Which formula is best for calculating 1RM?

No single formula is best for everyone. Epley and Brzycki are common choices. Comparing multiple estimates can provide a more useful range.

4. Can I calculate my bench press max?

Yes. Select Bench Press, enter the weight lifted and completed repetitions, then calculate your estimated one-rep maximum.

5. Can beginners use a 1RM calculator?

Beginners can use it to understand estimated strength, but should prioritize learning correct technique and building a consistent training base rather than testing maximum loads.

6. How many repetitions should I enter?

Sets of around 2–10 repetitions are commonly used for estimates. Predictions generally become less reliable as the repetition count increases.

7. Does this calculator work with pounds and kilograms?

Yes. Choose kilograms or pounds before entering your lifting weight. The result is displayed in the same unit.

8. Should I test my actual maximum lift?

You do not need to test a true maximum to estimate your strength. A completed submaximal set can provide an estimate without requiring an all-out attempt.
