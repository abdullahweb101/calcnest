What Is a Mortgage Calculator?

A mortgage calculator estimates the cost of financing a home. It helps homebuyers understand the monthly principal and interest payment and account for additional housing expenses such as property taxes and insurance.

Mortgage Payment Formula

M = P × [r(1 + r)n] ÷ [(1 + r)n − 1]
  • M: Monthly principal and interest payment
  • P: Mortgage loan principal
  • r: Monthly interest rate
  • n: Total number of monthly payments

The estimated monthly housing cost is the principal and interest payment plus any monthly property tax, home insurance, HOA fees and other costs entered.

Example Mortgage Calculation

Suppose a home costs $300,000 and you make a $60,000 down payment. Your mortgage principal is $240,000. At a fixed annual interest rate of 6.5% over 30 years, principal and interest are approximately $1,517 per month, before additional housing costs.

What Is a Down Payment?

A down payment is the amount you pay upfront when purchasing a property. A larger down payment reduces the amount you need to borrow and may affect your interest rate or mortgage insurance requirements.

What Costs Are Not Included?

Closing costs, lender fees, private mortgage insurance, maintenance surprises and variable-rate changes are not automatically included. Add applicable recurring expenses in the optional fields where appropriate.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What does a mortgage calculator calculate?

It estimates mortgage principal and interest payments and can include additional monthly housing expenses.

2. Does a bigger down payment reduce monthly payments?

Yes. A bigger down payment generally reduces the mortgage amount and the monthly principal and interest payment.

3. What is the difference between a mortgage and a home loan?

A home loan is money borrowed to purchase property. A mortgage is the loan arrangement secured against that property.

4. Does this calculator include property tax?

Yes. You can enter an estimated monthly property tax amount in the optional field.

5. Are mortgage results exact?

No. They are estimates. Actual lender terms, fees, insurance requirements and payment schedules can change your costs.
