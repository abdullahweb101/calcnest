What Is Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR)?

Basal Metabolic Rate, commonly known as BMR, is an estimate of the amount of energy your body uses to maintain essential functions while at complete rest. These functions include breathing, circulation, maintaining body temperature and supporting basic cellular processes.

Your body uses energy even when you are sleeping or sitting still. This resting energy requirement is an important part of total daily energy expenditure.

BMR is not the same as the total number of calories you burn in a day. Walking, exercise, digestion and other daily activities require additional energy.

How Does This BMR Calculator Work?

This calculator uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation. It estimates resting energy expenditure using four main inputs: weight, height, age and the sex-specific constant used by the equation.

Mifflin-St Jeor Formula for Men

BMR = (10 × Weight in kg) + (6.25 × Height in cm) − (5 × Age) + 5

Mifflin-St Jeor Formula for Women

BMR = (10 × Weight in kg) + (6.25 × Height in cm) − (5 × Age) − 161

When imperial measurements are selected, the calculator first converts pounds to kilograms and inches to centimetres. It then applies the same metric equation.

Example BMR Calculation

Consider a 30-year-old man who weighs 70 kg and is 175 cm tall.

BMR = (10 × 70) + (6.25 × 175) − (5 × 30) + 5
BMR = 700 + 1093.75 − 150 + 5
BMR = 1648.75 kcal/day

The estimated BMR in this example is approximately 1,649 calories per day. This is a calculated estimate, not a personalized diet target.

BMR vs TDEE: What Is the Difference?

BMR and Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE) are related but measure different things.

BMR

Estimates energy used for essential body functions while at rest.

TDEE

Estimates total daily energy use, including resting needs and activity.

Daily Activity

Walking, exercise, work and everyday movement contribute to energy use.

Factors That Can Affect BMR

Age

Energy requirements can change with age as body composition and other physiological factors change.

Body Size and Composition

Height, weight and the amount of metabolically active tissue can influence resting energy expenditure.

Hormones and Health

Hormonal conditions, illness, medications and other individual health factors may influence energy needs. A formula cannot account for every such factor.

Genetics and Individual Differences

People with similar age, height and weight may still have different resting energy requirements. Prediction equations provide estimates rather than direct measurements.

Frequently Asked Questions About BMR

1. What does BMR stand for?

BMR stands for Basal Metabolic Rate. It estimates the energy your body uses to maintain essential functions at rest.

2. How many calories does BMR represent?

BMR is expressed in calories per day and represents estimated resting energy expenditure. Your result depends on your measurements and the equation used.

3. Is BMR the same as maintenance calories?

No. Maintenance calories are more closely related to total daily energy expenditure, which includes physical activity and other energy needs beyond resting metabolism.

4. Which formula does this calculator use?

This calculator uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, a commonly used predictive equation for estimating resting energy expenditure in adults.

5. Does BMR include exercise?

No. Exercise and everyday movement are not included in a BMR estimate. They contribute to total daily energy expenditure.

6. Can I use BMR as my daily calorie intake goal?

No. BMR is not an individualized calorie intake recommendation. Personal energy needs depend on activity, health and other factors.

7. Is this BMR calculator accurate?

It provides an estimate using a published prediction equation. Actual resting energy expenditure can differ between individuals. A clinical measurement may be needed for a more direct assessment.

8. Can I calculate BMR in pounds and feet?

Yes. Select Imperial mode and enter your weight in pounds and height in feet and inches.
