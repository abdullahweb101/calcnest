What Is the Break-Even Point?

The break-even point is the sales level at which total revenue equals total costs. At this point, a business has covered its fixed and variable costs but has not made a profit or loss.

Understanding Fixed and Variable Costs

Fixed costs generally do not change directly with the number of units sold during the relevant period. Examples may include rent, certain salaries, insurance and software subscriptions.

Variable costs change with production or sales volume. Examples may include raw materials, packaging and per-unit manufacturing costs.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter total fixed costs for the period you are analysing.
  2. Enter the variable cost of producing or delivering one unit.
  3. Enter the selling price for one unit.
  4. Enter your expected sales volume to estimate the resulting profit or loss.
  5. Select your currency and calculate the break-even point.

How the Calculation Works

The contribution per unit is the selling price minus the variable cost per unit. The calculator divides total fixed costs by contribution per unit to estimate the break-even quantity, then rounds that quantity up to a whole unit.

Break-even revenue is the break-even quantity multiplied by the selling price. Estimated profit or loss at your planned sales volume is revenue minus fixed costs and variable costs for those units.

Important Assumptions

This is a simplified break-even model. It assumes a constant selling price, constant variable cost per unit and fixed costs that remain unchanged within the period. It does not automatically include taxes, discounts, multiple products, capacity limits, financing costs or changes in costs at different production levels.

Frequently Asked Questions

What happens below the break-even point?

If sales are below the break-even quantity, the business generally has not generated enough contribution to cover its fixed costs and may record an operating loss under this model.

What happens above the break-even point?

Once fixed costs are covered, additional units can contribute to profit, assuming the selling price and variable cost remain unchanged.

Can the break-even point be zero?

If fixed costs are zero and each unit has a positive contribution, the model calculates zero units as the break-even point.

What if the variable cost is higher than the selling price?

Each unit creates a negative contribution before fixed costs. Selling more units will increase the loss under these assumptions, so there is no break-even sales quantity.

Can I use this for services?

Yes. Treat one service delivery, booking or project as a unit, provided you can estimate its variable cost and selling price.
