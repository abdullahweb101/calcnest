What Is TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure)?

Total Daily Energy Expenditure, or TDEE, is an estimate of the total energy your body uses throughout an average day. It includes resting metabolism, physical activity, everyday movement and the energy required to digest food.

Your TDEE can be different from another person's even if you have similar body measurements. Activity patterns, body composition, age and individual physiology all contribute to energy needs.

A TDEE calculator provides a starting estimate. It cannot measure your exact daily calorie expenditure.

How Is TDEE Calculated?

This calculator first estimates your Basal Metabolic Rate using the Mifflin-St Jeor equation. It then multiplies that estimate by an activity factor to estimate total daily energy expenditure.

Step 1: Calculate BMR

Male: BMR = (10 × Weight kg) + (6.25 × Height cm) − (5 × Age) + 5

Female: BMR = (10 × Weight kg) + (6.25 × Height cm) − (5 × Age) − 161

Step 2: Apply the Activity Multiplier

TDEE = BMR × Activity Multiplier

Activity Multipliers Used

Activity Level Multiplier General Description
Sedentary 1.20 Little or no structured exercise
Lightly active 1.375 Light activity around 1–3 days/week
Moderately active 1.55 Moderate exercise around 3–5 days/week
Very active 1.725 Hard exercise around 6–7 days/week
Extra active 1.90 Very hard training or physically demanding work
Activity multipliers are approximate population-level assumptions. They do not precisely measure individual movement, exercise intensity or daily energy expenditure.

Example TDEE Calculation

Consider a 30-year-old man weighing 70 kg with a height of 175 cm. Assume he selects the moderately active multiplier of 1.55.

Step 1: BMR = 1,649 kcal/day (approximately)

Step 2: TDEE = 1,649 × 1.55

Estimated TDEE = 2,556 kcal/day

In this example, approximately 2,556 calories per day is the estimated energy expenditure. It is not a guaranteed maintenance intake for every individual with these measurements.

Understanding Maintenance Calories

Maintenance calories refer to an estimated average daily energy intake at which body weight may remain relatively stable over time. This is based on the principle of energy balance.

Energy Balance

Average energy intake and energy expenditure are broadly balanced over time.

Energy Deficit

Energy intake is below expenditure. Weight changes may occur, but responses vary.

Energy Surplus

Energy intake is above expenditure. Body weight and composition may change over time.

The result from this calculator is an estimate. Actual maintenance requirements can be evaluated more reliably by observing longer-term patterns and seeking professional guidance when needed.

Factors That Can Affect Your TDEE

Physical Activity

Exercise, walking, sports, active commuting and physically demanding work can all influence daily energy expenditure.

Body Composition

Differences in lean tissue and fat mass can affect resting energy requirements, even among people with similar body weights.

Age and Individual Physiology

Age, hormones, genetics, health conditions and other physiological factors may affect energy needs.

Daily Movement

Non-exercise activity such as standing, household tasks and walking also contributes to total daily energy expenditure.

Frequently Asked Questions About TDEE

1. What does TDEE stand for?

TDEE stands for Total Daily Energy Expenditure. It estimates the total energy your body uses in an average day.

2. What is the difference between BMR and TDEE?

BMR estimates resting energy expenditure. TDEE includes resting metabolism plus activity and other daily energy requirements.

3. Are TDEE maintenance calories exact?

No. They are estimates based on a prediction equation and an activity multiplier. Real energy requirements can vary.

4. Which activity level should I select?

Choose the description closest to your usual weekly activity. Consider your everyday movement as well as structured exercise.

5. Can TDEE change over time?

Yes. Changes in body weight, activity, age, health and other factors can change estimated daily energy expenditure.

6. Does TDEE include calories burned during exercise?

The activity multiplier is intended to account broadly for physical activity. It does not calculate an exact number of calories burned in an individual workout.

7. Is TDEE the same as calorie intake?

No. TDEE is estimated energy expenditure. Calorie intake is the energy you consume from food and drinks.

8. Can I use this calculator for children?

This calculator is designed for adults aged 18 and older. Children and teenagers have different nutritional and growth requirements and need appropriate professional assessment.
