What Is Simple Interest?

Simple interest is calculated only on the original principal amount. Unlike compound interest, it does not add previously earned interest to the principal for future interest calculations.

Simple Interest Formula

SI = (P × R × T) ÷ 100
  • SI: Simple interest
  • P: Principal amount
  • R: Annual interest rate in percent
  • T: Time in years

Total Amount Formula

A = P + SI

The total amount is the original principal plus the interest earned or charged over the selected period.

Simple Interest Example

Suppose you invest $10,000 at an annual simple interest rate of 5% for 3 years.

  • Principal: $10,000
  • Annual interest rate: 5%
  • Time: 3 years
  • Simple interest: $1,500
  • Total amount: $11,500

Simple Interest vs Compound Interest

Simple interest is always based on the original principal. Compound interest is calculated on the principal plus accumulated interest. Therefore, compound interest can grow faster over time.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. How do I calculate simple interest?

Multiply the principal by the annual interest rate and time in years, then divide by 100.

2. Does simple interest change every year?

For a fixed principal and rate, the interest earned each year remains the same.

3. Can I calculate interest for months?

Yes. Select months as the time unit. The calculator converts months into a fraction of a year.

4. What is the difference between interest and total amount?

Interest is the additional money earned or paid. Total amount includes the original principal plus interest.

5. Is simple interest the same as compound interest?

No. Simple interest is calculated on the original principal, while compound interest also includes previously accumulated interest.
