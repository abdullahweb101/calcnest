Where is power measured?

Electric motors, household appliances, generators, vehicles, engines, heating systems, industrial machines and power stations.

Power vs energy

Power describes how quickly energy is transferred. Energy describes the total amount transferred or consumed. Watts measure power; watt-hours measure energy.

Horsepower note: This converter distinguishes mechanical horsepower (hp), metric horsepower (PS) and boiler horsepower. These are different units.

Understanding common power units

Watt (W)

The watt is the SI derived unit of power. It measures the rate at which energy is transferred or used. One watt equals one joule per second. Small electronic devices and household equipment commonly display their power ratings in watts.

Kilowatt (kW)

A kilowatt equals 1,000 watts. It is commonly used to describe the power rating of appliances, motors, generators, electric vehicle chargers and industrial equipment. A 2 kW appliance has a rated power of 2,000 watts.

Megawatt (MW) and gigawatt (GW)

Megawatts and gigawatts represent large power levels. Power stations, electrical grids and major industrial installations commonly use these units. One megawatt equals one million watts, while one gigawatt equals one billion watts.

Mechanical horsepower (hp)

Mechanical horsepower is a traditional power unit often used to describe engines, motors and machinery. One mechanical horsepower equals approximately 745.699872 watts.

Metric horsepower (PS)

Metric horsepower is also known by the symbol PS. It is used in some countries for vehicle engine specifications. One metric horsepower equals approximately 735.49875 watts.

Boiler horsepower

Boiler horsepower is a specialized unit historically used to describe the steam-producing capacity of boilers. It is not the same as mechanical horsepower. One boiler horsepower is approximately 9,809.5 watts under the conventional definition.

BTU per hour (BTU/h)

BTU per hour measures the rate of heat energy transfer. It is commonly used in heating, ventilation and air conditioning specifications. It is a power unit, not simply an amount of energy.

Foot-pound force per second

This unit expresses mechanical power using foot-pound force per second. It is useful in certain engineering and mechanical calculations.

Power and energy: what is the difference?

Power and energy are related, but they describe different things. Power measures the rate at which energy is transferred or used. Energy measures the total amount transferred or consumed.

Energy = Power × Time

For example, a 1,000-watt appliance operating at its rated power for one hour uses 1,000 watt-hours, which equals 1 kilowatt-hour.

A kilowatt (kW) is a power unit, while a kilowatt-hour (kWh) is an energy unit. This distinction is especially important when reading electricity bills and comparing appliance specifications.

Where are power conversions useful?

Electrical appliances

Appliances such as heaters, refrigerators, washing machines and air conditioners often display power ratings in watts or kilowatts. Conversions help compare equipment from different manufacturers and regions.

Motors and industrial equipment

Industrial motors and machines may use horsepower or kilowatts in their specifications. Converting these units helps technicians and engineers compare ratings and understand equipment capacity.

Automobiles and engines

Vehicle manufacturers may publish engine output in horsepower, metric horsepower or kilowatts. Converting between these units makes specifications easier to compare.

Air conditioning and heating

Heating and cooling systems often use BTU per hour to describe their capacity. Converting BTU/h into watts or kilowatts helps compare equipment ratings expressed in different systems.

Renewable energy systems

Solar panels, inverters and generators may use watts and kilowatts to describe their rated output. Larger solar installations and power stations may use megawatts.
