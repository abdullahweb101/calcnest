Paint Calculation Formula

  • Wall area = Wall length × Wall height, added across selected walls.
  • Opening area = Doors area + Windows area.
  • Net wall area = Gross wall area − Opening area.
  • Ceiling area = Room length × Room width.
  • Paint area = Total paintable area × Number of coats.
  • Paint quantity = Paint area ÷ Effective coverage rate.
Always check the paint manufacturer's coverage information. Different paints and surfaces can produce different results.

Important Reminder

This tool estimates paint quantity for planning purposes. It does not replace the paint manufacturer's coverage instructions or a professional inspection of the surface.

How Much Paint Do You Need?

The amount of paint required depends on the total surface area, the number of coats, paint coverage, wall texture and the condition of the surface. A room with large windows and doors usually needs less wall paint than a room with the same dimensions and fewer openings.

The most reliable approach is to calculate each paintable surface, subtract openings, apply the number of coats and divide by the coverage rate printed on the paint container.

Understanding wall area

For a rectangular room with four walls, the combined wall area is calculated using the room perimeter multiplied by the wall height. If you only plan to paint one or two walls, select the corresponding number of walls and use the room dimensions as the basis for the estimate.

Four-wall area formula Gross wall area = 2 × (Room length + Room width) × Wall height

Subtracting doors and windows

Doors and windows are openings that generally do not need the same wall paint. Multiply the number of openings by the area of each opening, then subtract their combined area from the gross wall area.

Net wall area Net wall area = Gross wall area − Total door area − Total window area

Calculating paint for multiple coats

A single coat may not provide sufficient color coverage, especially when changing from a dark color to a lighter one or painting a porous surface. Select the number of coats recommended by the paint manufacturer.

Paint quantity Paint in liters = (Total paintable area × Number of coats) ÷ Coverage per liter

Why surface condition matters

A smooth, sealed wall may use less paint than an uneven, porous or previously unpainted surface. New plaster, repaired areas and dramatic color changes may also require primer or additional coats. Follow the preparation and application instructions for your specific product.

Paint quantity versus paint cost

The calculator can estimate the cost of the required paint using a price per liter. It also calculates the number of full cans to purchase based on your selected can size. The can calculation rounds upward because paint is generally purchased in complete containers.

Planning note: Paint estimates do not automatically include primer, ceilings unless enabled, trim, skirting boards, doors, furniture surfaces or special coatings. Calculate these separately when required.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. How do I calculate how much paint I need?

Calculate your paintable surface area, subtract openings, multiply by the number of coats and divide by the coverage per liter. Add a suitable allowance for wastage and touch-ups.

2. How much area does one liter of paint cover?

Coverage varies by product. Many wall paints provide a coverage figure around 8–12 m² per liter, but always use the manufacturer's stated rate.

3. How many coats of paint should I apply?

Two coats are common for many wall painting projects. The exact number depends on the surface, color change, product and manufacturer's guidance.

4. Should doors and windows be subtracted?

Yes. Enter the number and area of doors and windows to subtract their combined area from the wall surface.

5. Can I include ceiling paint?

Yes. Enable the ceiling option. The calculator adds room length multiplied by room width to the paintable surface area.

6. How much extra paint should I buy?

A modest allowance can help account for spills, application losses, surface variations and future touch-ups. The appropriate amount depends on the project.

7. Can I calculate the total paint cost?

Yes. Enter the price per liter and choose your currency. The calculator multiplies the required paint quantity by the entered price.

8. Can I use this calculator for exterior walls?

Yes. Enter the exterior wall dimensions and use the coverage rate specified for your exterior paint product.

9. Does rough wall texture use more paint?

Rough and porous surfaces can absorb or retain more paint. Use the appropriate product coverage and consider a lower effective coverage rate.

10. Are the results exact?

No. They are planning estimates. Actual consumption depends on paint quality, surface preparation, application technique and wall condition.
