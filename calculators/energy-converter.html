Where is energy measured?

Electricity consumption, food energy, heating and cooling, mechanical work, fuel energy, scientific experiments and industrial energy systems.

Energy vs power

Energy measures the amount of work or heat transferred. Power measures how quickly energy is transferred or used. A kilowatt is power; a kilowatt-hour is energy.

Unit note: This converter uses the thermochemical calorie and the international-table BTU. Food Calories are generally expressed as kilocalories (kcal).

Understanding common energy units

Joule (J)

The joule is the SI derived unit of energy. It is used in physics, engineering and scientific calculations. One joule is the work done when a force of one newton moves an object one meter in the direction of the force.

Kilojoule (kJ), megajoule (MJ) and gigajoule (GJ)

These units represent larger amounts of energy. A kilojoule equals 1,000 joules, a megajoule equals one million joules, and a gigajoule equals one billion joules. They are useful for comparing fuel energy, heat output and industrial energy.

Calorie and kilocalorie

Calories are units of energy often associated with food, nutrition and heat. One thermochemical calorie equals 4.184 joules. One kilocalorie equals 1,000 calories. On many food labels, the term Calories with a capital C refers to kilocalories.

Watt-hour and kilowatt-hour

Watt-hours and kilowatt-hours are widely used for electrical energy. One watt-hour is the energy transferred by a power of one watt over one hour. One kilowatt-hour equals 1,000 watt-hours and is commonly used on electricity bills.

British thermal unit (BTU)

BTU is a traditional unit of heat energy. It is frequently used in heating, ventilation and air-conditioning equipment specifications. Air conditioners and heating systems may use BTU-based ratings to describe heating or cooling capacity.

Electronvolt (eV)

Electronvolt is a very small energy unit used in atomic, particle and solid-state physics. It is particularly useful when describing the energy of electrons, photons and subatomic particles.

Foot-pound force

Foot-pound force is a unit of mechanical work or energy used in some engineering and mechanical contexts. It represents the work done by a force of one pound-force acting through a distance of one foot.

Energy conversion in everyday applications

Electricity consumption

Electricity providers commonly measure household and commercial energy consumption in kilowatt-hours. Converting kWh into joules or megajoules can help students, engineers and energy professionals compare measurements across different systems.

Food and nutrition

Food energy is commonly shown in kilocalories and kilojoules. Converting between these units helps compare nutrition information displayed using different measurement systems. This tool only converts energy units and does not provide dietary or medical recommendations.

Heating and cooling systems

Heating and cooling equipment often uses BTU-based specifications. Energy conversions help users understand heat-energy values expressed in joules, kilojoules or BTU. Remember that BTU is an energy unit, while BTU per hour is a power or heating/cooling rate.

Science and engineering

Scientific work may use joules, electronvolts and other specialized units. Engineers may compare mechanical work, electrical energy and heat using consistent SI measurements.

Frequently asked questions

1. How many joules are in one kilojoule?

One kilojoule equals exactly 1,000 joules.

2. How many joules are in one calorie?

One thermochemical calorie equals exactly 4.184 joules.

3. How many joules are in one kilowatt-hour?

One kilowatt-hour equals exactly 3,600,000 joules, or 3.6 megajoules.

4. What is the difference between a calorie and a kilocalorie?

One kilocalorie equals 1,000 small calories. Food labels often use Calories with a capital C to mean kilocalories.

5. How do you convert kWh to joules?

Multiply the kWh value by 3,600,000. For example, 2 kWh equals 7,200,000 joules.

6. What is BTU used for?

BTU is used to describe heat energy and commonly appears in heating, cooling and air-conditioning specifications.

7. What is an electronvolt?

An electronvolt is a small energy unit used in atomic and particle physics. One eV is approximately 1.602176634 × 10⁻¹⁹ joules.

8. Is a kilowatt the same as a kilowatt-hour?

No. A kilowatt (kW) measures power, while a kilowatt-hour (kWh) measures energy. One kilowatt-hour is the energy used by a 1 kW device operating for one hour.

9. What is the SI unit of energy?

The joule (J) is the SI derived unit of energy.

10. Is the CalcNest Energy Converter free?

Yes. It is free to use online and does not require registration or payment.
