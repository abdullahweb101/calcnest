What Is Cash Flow?

Cash flow describes the movement of money into and out of a business during a particular period. Cash inflows increase available cash, while cash outflows reduce it.

Three Main Types of Cash Flow

Operating cash flow: Cash generated or used by the core business activities, such as collecting customer payments and paying suppliers, employees and operating bills.

Investing cash flow: Cash movements related to buying or selling long-term assets and investments. Buying equipment is generally an outflow, while selling an asset may create an inflow.

Financing cash flow: Cash movements related to borrowing, repaying loans, owner contributions, share issues, dividends and withdrawals.

How Cash Flow Is Calculated

For each activity category, the calculator subtracts the listed cash outflows from the listed cash inflows. Net cash flow is the sum of operating, investing and financing cash flows.

Closing cash balance is calculated by adding net cash flow to the opening cash balance. The calculation is a simplified summary based on the cash movements you enter.

Cash Flow vs. Profit

Cash flow and accounting profit are not the same. A business may record revenue before receiving the cash, or record expenses that are not paid immediately. Loan principal repayments and asset purchases can also affect cash without being treated as ordinary operating expenses in the same way as other costs.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a profitable business have negative cash flow?

Yes. Timing differences, investment purchases, debt repayments or delayed customer payments can create negative cash flow even when a business reports accounting profit.

What does negative net cash flow mean?

It means the entered cash outflows exceed the entered cash inflows for the period. The business may use existing cash reserves or need additional funding.

Should loan repayments be included?

Loan principal repayments are included under financing activities in this simplified calculator. Interest classification can depend on the applicable accounting framework.

Can I use this for monthly cash flow?

Yes. Enter all values for the same month. You can also use it for a quarter or year, as long as the reporting period is consistent.

Is this a formal cash flow statement?

No. It is a planning calculator and simplified summary, not a complete financial statement prepared under an accounting standard.
