Tile Quantity Formula

  • Surface area = Length × Width.
  • Tile area = Tile length × Tile width.
  • Layout module = (Tile length + grout) × (Tile width + grout).
  • Base tiles = Net surface area ÷ Layout module.
  • Extra tiles = Base tiles × Waste percentage.
  • Boxes = Total tiles ÷ Tiles per box, rounded up.
The grout-adjusted module is a planning approximation. Actual tile layouts depend on edge cuts, joint alignment and installer measurements.

Suggested Extra Tile Allowance

  • Straight layout: often 5–10%.
  • Offset layout: often 10%.
  • Diagonal layout: often 10–15%.
  • Herringbone: often 10–15% or more.
  • Irregular spaces: assess individually.

These are general planning examples, not fixed installation rules.

Before Buying Tiles

Confirm tile dimensions, batch or shade consistency, package coverage, recommended joint width and installation requirements with your supplier or installer.

How to Calculate the Number of Tiles

To estimate tile requirements, first calculate the total surface area. Then determine the area of one tile and divide the surface area by the tile's effective layout area. The layout area can include the grout joint between adjacent tiles.

A rectangular room is straightforward to measure. For an L-shaped room, divide the surface into smaller rectangles, calculate each area and add them together. Subtract permanent areas that will not be tiled.

Rectangular surface area Surface area = Length × Width

Understanding grout spacing

Grout joints are the gaps between neighboring tiles. They help accommodate dimensional variation and movement within the tile installation system. The appropriate joint width depends on tile type, size, edge finish, manufacturer recommendations and applicable installation standards.

Approximate tile layout module Module length = Tile length + Grout joint
Module width = Tile width + Grout joint
Module area = Module length × Module width

This method is useful for estimating the number of tiles over a larger area. It does not replace a detailed tile layout plan. Perimeter cuts, corner arrangements and the position of the first tile can change the actual quantity.

Why should you buy extra tiles?

Tiles may need to be cut around walls, doors, pipes, drains and corners. Some pieces may break during handling or installation. Buying a suitable extra quantity helps prevent delays if more matching tiles are needed.

  • Simple rectangular rooms can have fewer cuts.
  • Diagonal installations generally create more offcuts.
  • Herringbone patterns require careful cutting and alignment.
  • Rooms with many corners and openings can need more spare tiles.
  • Keep matching spare tiles for future repairs when practical.

Calculating the number of tile boxes

Tile boxes normally contain a fixed number of pieces. Divide the total required tile count by the number of pieces in each box, then round up to a complete box.

Box quantity Boxes required = Total required tiles ÷ Tiles per box
Round the result up to a whole box.

Tile cost calculation

Tiles may be priced per square meter, per square foot or per box. If the supplier charges per square meter, multiply the required material area by the price per square meter. If the supplier charges per box, multiply the number of boxes by the price per box.

Material cost by area Material cost = Required tile area × Price per m²
Material cost by box Material cost = Number of boxes × Price per box

Different tile types

  • Ceramic tiles: Common for many indoor floors and walls. Check the product's intended use and water-resistance rating.
  • Porcelain tiles: Often selected for durability and lower water absorption. Follow the manufacturer's installation requirements.
  • Natural stone: Marble, granite and other stone products may need special installation and sealing.
  • Mosaic tiles: Often supplied on sheets, so calculate according to sheet coverage rather than treating every small piece as a separate package.
  • Large-format tiles: Require careful substrate preparation, handling and installation planning.

Important measurement tips

Measure the surface in more than one place if walls are not perfectly straight. Check the room's corners, doorways and any built-in features. For wall tiling, measure the height and width of each wall separately. For multiple surfaces, calculate each section independently.

Important: This tool provides an approximate tile count. A grout-adjusted area method does not calculate the exact physical layout or every edge cut. Confirm the final tile plan with your installer and check the manufacturer's packaging information before purchasing.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. How do I calculate how many tiles I need?

Calculate the surface area, divide it by the tile layout area and add an allowance for cutting and waste. The layout area can include the grout joint between tiles.

2. Should I include grout spacing?

Yes, grout spacing can be included in the layout module to improve a preliminary tile count. The actual installation layout may vary.

3. How much extra tile should I buy?

Around 5–10% is a common starting allowance for simple layouts. Complex patterns and irregular spaces may need more.

4. How many tiles are in one box?

It depends on tile dimensions and the manufacturer. Check the box for the piece count and total coverage.

5. Can I calculate wall tiles?

Yes. Enter wall length and height as your surface dimensions. You can also enter a known wall area manually.

6. What is a grout joint?

It is the gap between adjacent tiles. The joint is filled with grout and its width depends on the tile and installation system.

7. Does tile size affect the tile count?

Yes. Larger tiles generally cover more area per piece, so fewer pieces are needed for the same surface. Cutting and grout spacing also matter.

8. How do I calculate tile cost?

Multiply the required area by the price per square meter, or multiply the number of boxes by the price per box.

9. Does this calculator estimate grout quantity?

No. It uses grout joint width for an approximate tile layout calculation. Grout weight depends on joint depth, tile thickness, grout type and manufacturer specifications.

10. Are the results exact?

No. Results are estimates. Actual tile requirements depend on room shape, cutting, tile dimensions, joint width and installation conditions.
