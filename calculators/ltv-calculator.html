What Is Customer Lifetime Value (LTV)?

Customer Lifetime Value, also called CLV, estimates the total revenue or profit contribution a business can receive from a customer over the complete relationship. It is an important metric for ecommerce, subscription businesses, SaaS companies, retailers and service providers.

Instead of considering only the first purchase, LTV takes into account how much customers spend, how frequently they purchase and how long they remain active.

Example:

A customer spends an average of $100 per purchase, makes four purchases per year and remains a customer for three years. Estimated lifetime revenue is $1,200. At a 60% gross profit margin, estimated gross profit LTV is $720.

Customer Lifetime Value Formula

A simple transaction-based revenue formula is:

Revenue LTV = Average Purchase Value × Purchases Per Year × Customer Lifespan

Gross profit-based lifetime value is:

Gross Profit LTV = Revenue LTV × Gross Profit Margin

Worked Example

Assume the following business metrics:

  • Average purchase value: $100
  • Purchases per year: 4
  • Customer lifespan: 3 years
  • Gross profit margin: 60%
Revenue LTV = $100 × 4 × 3 = $1,200 Gross Profit LTV = $1,200 × 0.60 = $720

Subscription Business LTV Formula

Subscription companies often estimate customer lifespan using monthly churn. A simplified model assumes a stable recurring revenue amount and a constant churn rate.

Estimated Customer Lifespan in Months = 1 ÷ Monthly Churn Rate Revenue LTV = Monthly Revenue Per Customer ÷ Monthly Churn Rate Gross Profit LTV = (Monthly Revenue × Gross Margin) ÷ Monthly Churn Rate

For example, if monthly revenue is $50, monthly churn is 5% and gross profit margin is 80%:

Estimated Lifespan = 1 ÷ 0.05 = 20 months Revenue LTV = $50 ÷ 0.05 = $1,000 Gross Profit LTV = $1,000 × 0.80 = $800

This subscription formula is a simplified steady-state estimate. It does not model changing churn, expansion revenue, discounts, refunds or the time value of money.

Revenue LTV vs Gross Profit LTV

MetricMeaningExample
Revenue LTV Total estimated sales generated by a customer. $1,200
Gross Profit LTV Lifetime revenue after direct costs. $720 at 60% margin
Net Profit LTV Value after broader expenses and other relevant costs. Requires additional cost information

Revenue LTV is useful for understanding customer spending. Gross profit LTV is usually more appropriate for evaluating acquisition economics because it accounts for the direct cost of delivering products or services. Net profit LTV requires an even broader financial model.

Understanding the LTV to CAC Ratio

The LTV to CAC ratio compares the gross-profit-based lifetime value of a customer with the cost of acquiring that customer.

LTV to CAC Ratio = Gross Profit LTV ÷ Customer Acquisition Cost

For example:

$720 ÷ $150 = 4.8:1

This means the estimated gross profit lifetime value is 4.8 times the customer acquisition cost, before considering other operating expenses and financial timing.

How to Interpret the Ratio

RatioPossible Interpretation
Below 1:1Estimated gross profit LTV is below acquisition cost.
1:1Estimated gross profit LTV equals acquisition cost.
2:1Estimated gross profit LTV is twice acquisition cost.
3:1A commonly discussed reference point in some subscription businesses.
Above 3:1May indicate stronger acquisition economics, depending on other costs and timing.

These are illustrative interpretations, not universal performance standards. Business models, gross margins, retention and cash requirements can make the appropriate ratio very different.

What Is Break-Even Customer Acquisition Cost?

In a simplified model, gross profit LTV can be treated as the maximum acquisition cost before lifetime gross profit is fully consumed by customer acquisition spending.

Simplified Break-Even CAC = Gross Profit LTV

This is not a complete business break-even calculation. Businesses should also account for operating expenses, overhead, cash flow, discounts, refunds, time to recover spending and required profit.

Factors That Influence Customer Lifetime Value

Average Purchase Value

Customers who spend more per transaction generally produce more lifetime revenue, assuming purchase frequency and lifespan remain unchanged.

Purchase Frequency

Repeat purchases increase customer revenue over time. Loyalty programs, product quality and relevant follow-up offers can influence purchasing behavior.

Customer Retention

Customers who remain active longer may generate more revenue. Subscription cancellations and customer churn can reduce expected lifetime value.

Gross Profit Margin

A business with higher direct product or service costs may generate less gross profit from the same amount of customer revenue.

Customer Experience

Support quality, product reliability, delivery experience and customer satisfaction can influence repeat purchasing and retention.

Common LTV Calculation Mistakes

  • Using revenue LTV as if it were gross profit.
  • Ignoring refunds, returns, discounts and cancellations.
  • Using an unrealistic customer lifespan assumption.
  • Mixing monthly revenue with yearly churn or lifespan figures.
  • Comparing LTV and CAC calculated using incompatible customer groups.
  • Assuming historical purchase frequency will remain unchanged indefinitely.
  • Ignoring the time needed to recover customer acquisition spending.

Ways to Improve Customer Lifetime Value

  • Improve product quality and customer support.
  • Build customer loyalty and repeat-purchase programs.
  • Offer relevant complementary products or services.
  • Improve subscription retention and reduce avoidable churn.
  • Understand customer needs through feedback and behavior data.
  • Measure gross margin and customer value by customer segment.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What does LTV stand for?

LTV stands for Customer Lifetime Value. It estimates the revenue or profit contribution a business receives from a customer throughout the relationship.

2. Is LTV the same as CLV?

Yes. LTV and CLV are commonly used abbreviations for Customer Lifetime Value.

3. Should I use revenue LTV or gross profit LTV?

Revenue LTV measures customer sales. Gross profit LTV subtracts direct product or service costs and is generally more useful when comparing lifetime value with customer acquisition cost.

4. What is a good LTV to CAC ratio?

A 3:1 ratio is often discussed as a reference point in some subscription businesses. The right target depends on margins, retention, payback time, cash flow and business model.

5. How does churn affect subscription LTV?

Higher churn generally reduces expected customer lifespan and lifetime value. Lower churn can increase the period over which subscription revenue is generated.

6. Can ecommerce businesses calculate LTV?

Yes. Ecommerce businesses can estimate LTV using average order value, purchase frequency, customer lifespan and gross profit margin.

7. Does this calculator guarantee future customer revenue?

No. It estimates lifetime value using the assumptions entered. Actual revenue and profit may change due to churn, spending patterns, pricing, costs and customer behavior.

8. What is break-even CAC?

In a simplified gross-profit model, break-even CAC equals gross profit LTV. A complete business decision must also consider operating expenses, cash flow and the desired profit.

9. Can LTV be calculated for a one-time purchase business?

Yes. If customers usually purchase once, average purchase value and gross profit margin can provide a simple estimate. Repeat purchases should only be included when supported by data.
