Pressure measurement

Pressure describes the force applied over a particular area. The SI unit of pressure is the Pascal (Pa).

Safety note: This calculator only converts units. It does not determine the correct pressure for a vehicle, machine, medical device or industrial system. Always follow the relevant manufacturer's or professional guidance.

Pressure conversion formulas

The calculator converts all selected pressure units through Pascal (Pa), the SI derived unit of pressure. It first converts the input into Pascals and then calculates the target unit.

Pressure in Pa = Input value × Pascal factor of source unit

Converted pressure = Pressure in Pa ÷ Pascal factor of target unit

Useful conversion formulas

PSI to kPa = PSI × 6.894757293

Bar to kPa = Bar × 100

kPa to PSI = kPa ÷ 6.894757293

MPa to bar = MPa × 10

atm to Pa = atm × 101,325

Understanding pressure measurement units

Pascal (Pa)

Pascal is the International System of Units (SI) measurement for pressure. One Pascal equals one Newton of force applied over one square meter. It is widely used in engineering, science and technical calculations.

Kilopascal (kPa) and Megapascal (MPa)

A kilopascal equals 1,000 Pascals, while a megapascal equals 1,000,000 Pascals. These units are useful for measuring atmospheric pressure, material strength, hydraulic systems and industrial equipment.

Bar and millibar

Bar is a metric unit commonly used in industrial applications, compressors, pressure equipment and some tyre pressure specifications. One bar equals 100,000 Pascals. One millibar equals 100 Pascals.

PSI (pounds per square inch)

PSI is a pressure unit commonly used in the United States and other regions. It is frequently seen on tyre gauges, air compressors, pressure washers and mechanical equipment. One PSI is approximately 6.89476 kPa.

Atmosphere (atm)

Standard atmosphere is a reference pressure unit based on the defined standard atmospheric pressure of 101,325 Pascals. It is used in scientific and educational contexts.

Millimeters of mercury (mmHg) and torr

Millimeters of mercury are used in medical and scientific measurements. Blood pressure is commonly expressed in mmHg. Torr is another pressure unit with a value very close to one millimeter of mercury.

Inches of mercury and water column

Inches of mercury are used in some pressure gauges and vacuum measurements. Inches and centimeters of water column are used in low-pressure applications, including some HVAC and ventilation systems.

Pressure conversion in real-world applications

Automotive tyre pressure

Vehicle tyre pressure may be displayed in PSI, bar or kPa, depending on the country, vehicle and gauge. This converter helps you understand the relationship between these units. Always use the tyre pressure recommended by the vehicle manufacturer rather than choosing a pressure based on a conversion example.

Industrial and hydraulic equipment

Engineers and technicians may work with PSI, bar, kPa and MPa when reading equipment specifications, pressure gauges and hydraulic system documentation. Accurate unit conversion helps compare measurements expressed in different systems.

Weather and atmospheric pressure

Weather reports commonly use hectopascals or millibars to describe atmospheric pressure. One hPa equals one mbar, and both are equal to 100 Pascals.

Medical and laboratory measurements

mmHg is widely used in blood pressure reporting, while other pressure units may appear in laboratory equipment and scientific instruments. This converter is for unit conversion only and should not be used to interpret or diagnose a medical condition.

Frequently asked questions

1. How many PSI are in one bar?

One bar is approximately equal to 14.5038 PSI.

2. How many kPa are in one bar?

One bar is exactly equal to 100 kilopascals.

3. What is the difference between PSI and bar?

PSI means pounds per square inch, while bar is a metric pressure unit. Both are used to measure pressure in tyres, equipment and industrial systems.

4. What is standard atmospheric pressure?

Standard atmospheric pressure is exactly 101,325 Pa. It is equal to 101.325 kPa, 1.01325 bar and approximately 14.6959 PSI.

5. How do I convert PSI to kPa?

Multiply the PSI value by approximately 6.894757. For example, 30 PSI is approximately 206.84 kPa.

6. What is mmHg used for?

Millimeters of mercury are commonly used in medical pressure measurements, including blood pressure, and in scientific applications.

7. Is bar the same as atmosphere?

No. One bar equals 100,000 Pa, while one standard atmosphere equals 101,325 Pa.

8. What is the SI unit of pressure?

The Pascal (Pa) is the SI derived unit of pressure. One Pascal is equal to one Newton per square meter.

9. Are mmHg and torr exactly the same?

They are very close but are not strictly identical under their standard definitions. This calculator uses separate conversion factors for both units.

10. Is the CalcNest Pressure Converter free?

Yes. It is free to use online and does not require registration or payment.
