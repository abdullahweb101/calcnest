How Fuel Economy Conversion Works

Fuel economy describes how far a vehicle travels using a quantity of fuel, or how much fuel it consumes over a given distance.

km/L and L/100 km

These two metric measurements are reciprocals. A higher km/L value means better fuel economy, while a lower L/100 km value means lower fuel consumption.

L/100 km = 100 ÷ km/L
km/L = 100 ÷ L/100 km

US MPG and UK MPG

US and UK MPG are not the same because the US gallon is smaller than the imperial gallon. The converter keeps both gallon standards separate.

Examples

10 km/L = 10 L/100 km
10 km/L ≈ 23.52 US MPG
30 US MPG ≈ 7.84 L/100 km

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I convert km/L to L/100 km?

Divide 100 by the km/L value. For example, 10 km/L equals 10 L/100 km.

How do I convert L/100 km to km/L?

Divide 100 by the L/100 km value. For example, 8 L/100 km equals 12.5 km/L.

What is the difference between US MPG and UK MPG?

They use different gallon sizes. One imperial gallon is larger than one US gallon, so the same vehicle can have a higher numerical UK MPG figure than US MPG.

Is higher MPG better?

Yes. A higher MPG value means the vehicle travels more miles using one gallon of fuel.

Is lower L/100 km better?

Yes. A lower L/100 km figure means the vehicle uses less fuel to travel 100 kilometers.

Does this converter calculate actual fuel costs?

No. It converts fuel economy measurements. To calculate trip costs, you also need distance travelled and fuel price.
