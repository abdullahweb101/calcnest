Understanding MPG

MPG means miles per gallon. It measures how far a vehicle can travel using one gallon of fuel. A higher MPG number generally means better fuel economy.

Understanding L/100km

L/100km means liters per 100 kilometers. It measures how much fuel a vehicle consumes over a fixed distance. A lower number generally means better fuel economy.

Important: US MPG and UK MPG are different because the US liquid gallon and Imperial gallon have different volumes. Always select the correct MPG system.

km/L = US MPG ÷ 2.352145833

Because L/100km measures fuel consumed over a fixed distance, its relationship with km/L and MPG is reciprocal. This means you divide by the value rather than multiply when converting between these consumption and distance-per-fuel units.

Understanding common fuel economy units

US miles per gallon (US MPG)

US MPG measures the number of miles a vehicle can travel using one US liquid gallon of fuel. It is commonly used in the United States and appears in vehicle fuel economy ratings and automotive comparisons.

One US liquid gallon equals exactly 3.785411784 liters. Because this gallon is smaller than an Imperial gallon, US MPG figures are numerically lower than UK MPG figures for the same underlying fuel efficiency.

UK miles per gallon (Imperial MPG)

UK MPG measures the distance traveled in miles using one Imperial gallon of fuel. It is used in the United Kingdom and in some other contexts where Imperial measurements are used.

One Imperial gallon equals exactly 4.54609 liters. It is larger than the US liquid gallon, which is why the same vehicle can have a higher numerical UK MPG value than its US MPG value.

Kilometers per liter (km/L)

Kilometers per liter measures how many kilometers a vehicle can travel using one liter of fuel. It is used in various countries and is convenient for comparing distance traveled per unit of fuel.

Liters per 100 kilometers (L/100km)

L/100km measures how many liters of fuel a vehicle consumes to travel 100 kilometers. It is widely used in countries that use the metric system.

Unlike MPG and km/L, a lower L/100km value generally indicates better fuel economy because the vehicle uses less fuel to travel the same distance.

Why US MPG and UK MPG are different

The United States and the United Kingdom use different gallon sizes. The US liquid gallon is approximately 3.785 liters, while the Imperial gallon is approximately 4.546 liters.

Since an Imperial gallon contains more fuel, a vehicle can travel the same distance using one Imperial gallon and receive a higher numerical MPG value.

1 UK MPG ≈ 1.20095 US MPG
1 US MPG ≈ 0.83267 UK MPG

These relationships are important when comparing vehicle specifications published in different countries. Always check whether an MPG figure is based on the US gallon or the Imperial gallon.

What affects vehicle fuel economy?

Driving speed

Driving speed can significantly affect fuel consumption. Fuel economy may decrease when driving at high speeds because aerodynamic drag increases as speed rises.

Acceleration and braking

Frequent rapid acceleration and hard braking can increase fuel use. Smooth driving and maintaining a consistent speed may help improve efficiency.

Vehicle weight

Carrying unnecessary weight can increase the energy required to move a vehicle. Heavier vehicles may consume more fuel than comparable lighter vehicles, depending on their design and driving conditions.

Tire pressure

Incorrect tire pressure can increase rolling resistance and affect fuel consumption. Following the vehicle manufacturer's recommended tire pressure is important.

Traffic and road conditions

Stop-and-go traffic, steep roads, poor road surfaces and frequent short journeys can affect real-world fuel economy.

Vehicle maintenance

Regular maintenance, appropriate engine oil and properly maintained filters and tires can help a vehicle operate as intended.

Frequently asked questions

1. What is the difference between MPG and L/100km?

MPG measures distance traveled per gallon of fuel. L/100km measures liters consumed over 100 kilometers. Higher MPG generally means better fuel economy, while lower L/100km generally means better economy.

2. What is the difference between US MPG and UK MPG?

US MPG uses the smaller US liquid gallon, while UK MPG uses the larger Imperial gallon. The same vehicle therefore has different numerical values in the two systems.

3. How do you convert US MPG to L/100km?

Divide 235.214583 by the US MPG value. For example, 30 US MPG is approximately 7.84 L/100km.

4. How do you convert UK MPG to L/100km?

Divide approximately 282.480936 by the UK MPG value. For example, 50 UK MPG is approximately 5.65 L/100km.

5. How do you convert km/L to L/100km?

Divide 100 by the km/L value. For example, 12.5 km/L equals 8 L/100km.

6. Is higher MPG better?

Generally, yes. A higher MPG figure means a vehicle travels farther using the same amount of fuel. Lower L/100km generally indicates better fuel economy.

7. What is a good fuel economy?

Fuel economy depends on vehicle size, engine, fuel type, driving conditions and testing standards. Compare similar vehicles under comparable conditions for a meaningful assessment.

8. Is the CalcNest Fuel Economy Converter free?

Yes. It is free to use online and does not require registration.

9. Can I convert US MPG directly to UK MPG?

Yes. Select US MPG as the original unit and UK MPG as the destination unit. The converter accounts for the different gallon sizes.

10. Does this calculator estimate my actual fuel consumption?

No. It converts a fuel economy figure you provide. Actual vehicle fuel consumption depends on driving conditions, vehicle condition, fuel type and other factors.
