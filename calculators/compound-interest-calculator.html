What Is Compound Interest?

Compound interest is interest calculated on both the original principal and the interest accumulated during previous periods. This means your investment can grow faster over time because previously earned interest also starts earning interest.

Compound interest is commonly used when estimating savings growth, investment returns and the future value of money. The final amount depends on the starting balance, interest rate, investment period and how frequently interest is compounded.

Compound Interest Formula

The standard compound interest formula for a fixed principal and constant interest rate is:

A = P × (1 + r/n)nt
  • A = Future value of the investment
  • P = Initial principal amount
  • r = Annual interest rate in decimal form
  • n = Number of compounding periods per year
  • t = Investment period in years

To calculate the interest earned, subtract the initial principal from the future value:

Compound Interest = A − P

Why Does Compounding Frequency Matter?

Compounding frequency describes how often earned interest is added to the principal. Depending on the rate and terms, more frequent compounding generally produces a slightly higher future value.

  • Annual: Interest is compounded once per year.
  • Quarterly: Interest is compounded four times per year.
  • Monthly: Interest is compounded twelve times per year.
  • Daily: Interest is compounded 365 times per year.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is the difference between simple and compound interest?

Simple interest is calculated only on the original principal. Compound interest is calculated on the principal plus previously accumulated interest.

2. Does compound interest increase money automatically?

Compound interest can increase an account balance when interest is earned and retained in the account. Actual growth depends on the account terms, rates, fees and other conditions.

3. Is monthly compounding better than annual compounding?

With the same nominal annual rate and positive principal, monthly compounding generally results in a higher calculated amount than annual compounding.

4. Can I use this calculator for savings?

Yes. You can use it to estimate the future value of a fixed starting savings balance when the annual rate and compounding frequency are known.

5. Does this calculator include monthly deposits?

No. This version calculates compound growth on an initial principal without additional deposits. Regular contributions require a different calculation.
