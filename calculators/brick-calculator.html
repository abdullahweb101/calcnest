How to Calculate the Number of Bricks for a Wall

The number of bricks needed depends on the size of the wall, its thickness, the size of each brick and the mortar joint used between bricks. Doors, windows and other openings should also be deducted from the wall area.

A useful way to estimate bricks for a wall is to calculate the net wall volume and divide it by the modular volume of one brick. Modular dimensions include the mortar joint, because the joint occupies space between bricks.

Gross Wall Area = Wall Length × Wall Height × Number of Walls
Net Wall Area = Gross Wall Area − Total Opening Area
Net Wall Volume = Net Wall Area × Wall Thickness
Modular Brick Length = Brick Length + Mortar Joint
Modular Brick Height = Brick Height + Mortar Joint
Modular Brick Depth = Brick Depth + Mortar Joint
Bricks Required = Net Wall Volume ÷ Modular Brick Volume
Final Bricks = Round Up(Bricks Required × (1 + Waste Percentage))

All dimensions must be converted to the same unit before calculating volume. This calculator converts wall measurements and brick dimensions to meters internally so that the calculation remains consistent.

Why Wall Thickness Matters

Wall thickness affects the volume of masonry and the way bricks are laid. A thicker wall generally requires more bricks than a thinner wall of the same length and height.

  • Half-brick wall: A relatively thin masonry wall, often used for partitions in some construction systems.
  • Single-brick wall: A thicker wall configuration that uses a different brick arrangement.
  • Double-brick or cavity construction: May require separate calculations for multiple masonry leaves and the cavity.

These terms vary by country and building practice. Enter the actual thickness of the masonry being estimated rather than relying only on a wall-type name.

How Doors and Windows Affect Brick Quantity

A wall may include windows, doors, vents or other openings. These spaces do not require the same quantity of wall bricks, so their combined area should be subtracted from the gross wall area.

Opening Area = Opening Width × Opening Height

For multiple openings, calculate each opening area and add them together. For example, two windows measuring 1.2 m by 1.0 m have a combined area of 2.4 m².

Structural elements such as lintels, reinforced concrete columns and special masonry details are not included in this simple wall estimate.

Understanding Mortar Joints

Mortar joints are the spaces between bricks that are filled with mortar. Joint thickness changes the number of bricks that fit into a given wall volume.

A larger joint increases the modular dimensions of each brick and can reduce the estimated brick count for a fixed wall volume. The correct joint size depends on the brick type, mortar specification and construction method.

  • Measure the actual brick dimensions rather than relying on nominal sizes.
  • Use the joint thickness specified for your masonry work.
  • Keep horizontal and vertical joints consistent where the bond requires it.
  • Do not use the calculator's theoretical mortar volume as a direct order quantity.

How Much Brick Wastage Should You Add?

Some bricks may break during transport, handling or cutting. Additional bricks may also be needed around corners, openings and wall intersections. A waste allowance helps account for these uncertainties.

  • 3%: A small planning allowance for straightforward work with careful handling.
  • 5%: A commonly used preliminary estimate for relatively simple projects.
  • 7–10%: May be considered when more cutting or handling loss is expected.
  • 15%: May be considered for unusual layouts or difficult site conditions.

These percentages are examples for planning, not universal construction standards. Ask your supplier or builder what allowance is appropriate.

What About Mortar and Cement Requirements?

This calculator estimates the theoretical mortar space between bricks based on modular brick dimensions and wall volume. It does not calculate a complete mortar mix or the number of cement bags to purchase.

Actual mortar quantities depend on joint thickness, brick absorption, workmanship, mortar mix proportions, site losses and the type of masonry. Cement and sand requirements should be calculated separately using the specified mortar mix.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. How many bricks do I need for a wall?

The quantity depends on wall length, height, thickness, brick dimensions, mortar joints and openings. Enter those details in the calculator to estimate the required number of bricks.

2. Does this calculator support meters and feet?

Yes. Wall dimensions can be entered in meters or feet. Brick dimensions, wall thickness and mortar joints have their own unit selections.

3. Does the calculator subtract windows and doors?

Yes. Enter the combined area of all wall openings. The calculator subtracts this area before estimating the net wall volume.

4. Are mortar joints included in the brick count?

Yes. The calculator adds the selected joint thickness to each brick dimension to estimate its modular volume.

5. Can I calculate bricks for multiple walls?

Yes. Enter the number of identical walls. The total opening area should represent the combined openings across all those walls.

6. What if my walls have different dimensions?

Calculate each different wall separately, then add the results. Alternatively, group walls only when their dimensions and specifications are the same.

7. How much extra material should I buy?

A preliminary allowance of 5% may be a reasonable starting point for some straightforward projects. The correct allowance depends on brick quality, handling, cutting and site conditions.

8. Does the calculator estimate mortar quantity?

It provides a theoretical mortar-space estimate based on the entered dimensions. Actual mortar purchasing quantities require a separate mix and site-waste calculation.

9. Can I calculate the price of all required bricks?

Yes. Enter the price per brick and select your currency. The calculator multiplies the price by the estimated number of bricks, including wastage.

10. Is this calculator suitable for structural design?

No. It estimates material quantities only. Structural design, wall stability, foundations, reinforcement and building-code compliance must be assessed by qualified professionals.
