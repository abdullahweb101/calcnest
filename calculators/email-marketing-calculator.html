What Are Email Marketing Metrics?

Email marketing metrics are measurements used to understand how recipients respond to email campaigns. They help businesses evaluate delivery, engagement, audience quality, conversions and revenue.

A campaign can have a large number of opens but few clicks, or many clicks but very few purchases. Looking at multiple metrics provides a more useful picture than relying on one number alone.

Email Marketing Formulas

1. Email Open Rate

Open Rate = (Unique Opens ÷ Delivered Emails) × 100

Open rate measures the proportion of delivered emails recorded as opened. It is an engagement indicator, but tracking technology and privacy features can affect its accuracy.

2. Email Click-Through Rate (CTR)

Email CTR = (Unique Clicks ÷ Delivered Emails) × 100

Click-through rate measures the percentage of delivered emails that generated at least one recorded unique click. It helps assess whether email content and calls to action encourage recipients to visit a website or landing page.

3. Click-to-Open Rate (CTOR)

CTOR = (Unique Clicks ÷ Unique Opens) × 100

Click-to-open rate compares clicks with recorded opens. It can help evaluate how effectively the email content encourages action among recipients whose opens were recorded.

4. Email Bounce Rate

Bounce Rate = (Bounced Emails ÷ Emails Sent) × 100

Bounce rate measures emails that could not be delivered. A hard bounce is generally a permanent delivery failure, while a soft bounce is often temporary.

5. Unsubscribe Rate

Unsubscribe Rate = (Unsubscribes ÷ Delivered Emails) × 100

Unsubscribe rate measures the percentage of delivered recipients who opted out. Consistent reporting definitions are important because platforms may use different denominators.

6. Email Conversion Rate

Conversion Rate = (Attributed Conversions ÷ Delivered Emails) × 100

A conversion may be a purchase, registration, download, booking or another action defined by the business. The result depends on the attribution window and tracking rules used.

7. Revenue Per Email Sent

Revenue Per Email Sent = Campaign Revenue ÷ Emails Sent

8. Revenue Per Email Delivered

Revenue Per Email Delivered = Campaign Revenue ÷ Delivered Emails

9. Email Campaign Return on Investment

Campaign ROI = ((Campaign Revenue − Campaign Cost) ÷ Campaign Cost) × 100

This simplified ROI formula uses revenue and campaign cost. It does not account for product costs or all other business expenses. For a profit-based ROI, use the relevant profit figure instead of revenue.

What Is a Good Email Marketing Rate?

There is no single performance benchmark that applies to every business. Email results can vary by industry, audience, list age, campaign purpose, sending frequency, season and tracking method.

Instead of treating a universal percentage as a guaranteed target, compare campaigns with similar audiences and objectives. Monitor trends over time and focus on conversions and business outcomes as well as engagement.

How to Improve Email Campaign Performance

Build a Relevant Email List

Use permission-based signup methods and send messages that match subscriber interests. Avoid purchased or scraped lists.

Write Clear Subject Lines

Subject lines should accurately describe the email and help recipients understand why the message matters to them.

Segment Your Audience

Group subscribers by relevant characteristics such as interests, purchase history or engagement. Send content that is useful for each segment.

Improve Calls to Action

Use clear links and buttons that explain the next step. Make sure the landing page matches the promise made in the email.

Test Mobile Layouts

Many recipients read email on mobile devices. Use readable text, accessible buttons and a layout that works on smaller screens.

Maintain List Quality

Monitor hard bounces, remove invalid addresses and follow applicable consent and unsubscribe requirements.

Important Limitations of Email Tracking

  • Email privacy features may automatically load tracking pixels and affect recorded open rates.
  • Some email clients block images, so genuine opens may not be recorded.
  • Security systems may automatically scan links and generate clicks that are not human engagement.
  • Different platforms may define delivered emails, unique clicks and unsubscribes differently.
  • Attribution windows can change the number of conversions credited to an email campaign.
  • Revenue attributed to email does not always mean the email caused the entire purchase.

For better decisions, use a combination of clicks, verified website sessions, conversions, revenue and customer behavior rather than relying on open rate alone.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is email open rate?

Open rate is the percentage of delivered emails recorded as opened. It is calculated by dividing unique opens by delivered emails and multiplying by 100.

2. What is a good email click-through rate?

A good CTR depends on the industry, campaign type, audience and offer. Compare similar campaigns over time instead of relying on one universal benchmark.

3. What is the difference between CTR and CTOR?

CTR compares unique clicks with delivered emails. CTOR compares unique clicks with recorded unique opens.

4. How is email bounce rate calculated?

Divide bounced emails by total emails sent and multiply by 100. This calculator uses total sent emails as the bounce-rate denominator.

5. What does a high unsubscribe rate mean?

It may indicate that recipients find the email frequency, content or relevance unsuitable. Review audience expectations, signup consent and campaign segmentation.

6. How do I calculate email conversion rate?

Divide conversions attributed to the campaign by delivered emails and multiply by 100. Define the conversion event and attribution window before comparing results.

7. Why are email open rates sometimes inaccurate?

Privacy features, image proxying, blocked tracking pixels and automated systems can change how opens are recorded. Treat open rates as an approximate engagement indicator.

8. What is revenue per email?

It is campaign revenue divided by the number of emails sent or delivered. Always state which denominator is being used.

9. Does email campaign ROI equal profit margin?

No. The simplified campaign ROI formula compares revenue with campaign cost. Profit margin uses a different calculation and should account for the relevant costs.
