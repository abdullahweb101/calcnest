What Is Return on Ad Spend (ROAS)?

Return on Ad Spend (ROAS) is a marketing performance metric used to evaluate how much revenue an advertising campaign generates compared with the money spent on that campaign. Businesses use it to assess paid search, social media advertising, display campaigns, shopping ads and other paid marketing activities.

ROAS is usually shown as a ratio such as 2x, 3x or 5x. A 5x ROAS means that the campaign generated five units of attributed revenue for every one unit spent on advertising. It does not mean that the business earned five units of net profit.

ROAS Calculator Formula

ROAS = Advertising Revenue ÷ Advertising Spend

To express ROAS as a percentage, multiply the ratio by 100. For example, a 4x ROAS is equivalent to 400% revenue relative to ad spend.

Other Important ROAS Formulas

ACOS (%) = (Advertising Spend ÷ Advertising Revenue) × 100
Revenue After Ad Spend = Advertising Revenue − Advertising Spend
Estimated Gross Profit = Advertising Revenue × Gross Margin Rate
Estimated Profit After Ads = Estimated Gross Profit − Advertising Spend
Break-Even ROAS = 1 ÷ Gross Margin Rate

The break-even formula above is a simplified estimate. It assumes that the entered gross margin is the amount available to cover advertising spend and that other variable and fixed costs are excluded.

ROAS Calculation Examples

These examples show how different campaign results affect advertising return.

Example 1: 5x ROAS

Advertising revenue: $5,000

Advertising spend: $1,000

5,000 ÷ 1,000 = 5x ROAS

Each $1 spent generated $5 in attributed revenue.

Example 2: 2x ROAS

Advertising revenue: $2,000

Advertising spend: $1,000

2,000 ÷ 1,000 = 2x ROAS

Each $1 spent generated $2 in attributed revenue.

Example 3: Below 1x ROAS

Advertising revenue: $800

Advertising spend: $1,000

800 ÷ 1,000 = 0.8x ROAS

Attributed revenue was lower than advertising spend.

Example 4: Break-Even ROAS

Gross margin: 50%

Break-even ROAS: 1 ÷ 0.50

Break-even ROAS = 2x

At this simplified threshold, gross profit equals ad spend.

What Is a Good ROAS?

There is no universal ROAS target that works for every business. A campaign with a 2x ROAS may be profitable for a high-margin product, while a 5x ROAS may still be insufficient for a business with narrow margins and high fulfilment costs.

Set your target by considering product costs, gross margin, payment fees, shipping, refunds, agency fees, overhead and the purpose of the campaign. Compare results using consistent revenue attribution rules.

ROAS vs ROI

ROAS focuses on attributed advertising revenue compared with ad spend. ROI is a broader measure that compares profit or return with the investment made. A ROAS report can help evaluate advertising efficiency, but a full business profitability analysis needs more cost information.

ROAS vs ACOS

ROAS and Advertising Cost of Sales (ACOS) describe related campaign performance from opposite directions. ROAS divides advertising revenue by ad spend, while ACOS divides ad spend by advertising revenue. For the same non-zero values, a 4x ROAS corresponds to a 25% ACOS.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What does ROAS stand for?

ROAS stands for Return on Ad Spend. It measures attributed advertising revenue relative to advertising cost.

2. How do I calculate ROAS manually?

Divide attributed advertising revenue by advertising spend. For example, $6,000 in revenue divided by $1,500 in spend equals 4x ROAS.

3. Is 3x ROAS good?

It depends on your gross margin and other expenses. A 3x return may be profitable for one business and unprofitable for another.

4. What is a break-even ROAS?

It is the ROAS needed to cover the costs included in your model. With a 50% gross margin and no other costs considered, the simplified break-even ROAS is 2x.

5. Can ROAS be negative?

Standard ROAS cannot be negative when revenue and ad spend are non-negative. It can be zero when there is no attributed revenue and positive when revenue is generated.

6. Does ROAS measure profit?

No. Standard ROAS measures revenue relative to ad spend. It does not automatically subtract product costs, fulfilment, fees, taxes or overhead.

7. What is the difference between ROAS and ACOS?

ROAS is revenue divided by advertising spend. ACOS is advertising spend divided by advertising revenue, expressed as a percentage.

8. Why should I use the same attribution period?

Comparing campaigns with different attribution windows can make results misleading. Use consistent reporting periods and attribution rules wherever possible.

9. Does this calculator include operating expenses?

No. Its estimated profit subtracts advertising spend from estimated gross profit. Add other business costs separately for a fuller profitability assessment.

10. Can I use this calculator for Google Ads and social media ads?

Yes. You can use it for any advertising platform as long as revenue and spend refer to the same campaign, currency and reporting period.
