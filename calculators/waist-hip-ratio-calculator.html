What Is Waist-to-Hip Ratio (WHR)?

Waist-to-hip ratio, commonly abbreviated as WHR, is a simple measurement that compares waist circumference with hip circumference. It is used as one indicator of how body fat is distributed.

Unlike body weight or BMI, WHR focuses on the relationship between two circumference measurements. It can provide additional information about abdominal fat distribution, which is relevant in population-level health research.

Remember: WHR is a screening measure. It cannot tell you exactly how much body fat you have or diagnose a health condition.

Waist-to-Hip Ratio Formula

The calculation is straightforward:

Waist-to-Hip Ratio = Waist Circumference ÷ Hip Circumference

Example Calculation

Suppose a person has a waist measurement of 80 cm and a hip measurement of 100 cm.

WHR = 80 ÷ 100

WHR = 0.80

The result is a ratio, not a percentage. A value of 0.80 means the waist measurement is 80% of the hip measurement.

General Waist-to-Hip Ratio Reference Thresholds

The World Health Organization has published commonly used WHR thresholds for identifying increased risk associated with abdominal fat distribution.

Reference category WHR threshold
Men 0.90 or higher
Women 0.85 or higher

These are commonly cited screening thresholds, not a complete classification system for every person. A result below a threshold does not guarantee good health, and a result at or above it does not establish that a disease is present.

Age, ethnicity, pregnancy, health history, and other factors may affect how measurements should be interpreted.

How to Measure Your Waist Correctly

  1. Stand upright and breathe normally.
  2. Use a flexible, non-stretch measuring tape.
  3. Locate the midpoint between the bottom of your lowest rib and the top of your hip bone.
  4. Wrap the tape horizontally around your waist at that point.
  5. Keep the tape snug without compressing your skin.
  6. Record the measurement after a normal exhalation.

Consistency matters. Use the same measurement location and technique whenever you repeat the measurement.

How to Measure Your Hip Circumference

  1. Stand with your feet together and your weight evenly distributed.
  2. Wrap the measuring tape around the widest part of your hips and buttocks.
  3. Keep the tape level and parallel to the floor.
  4. Make sure the tape is snug but does not compress the body.
  5. Record the measurement using the same unit as your waist measurement.

Waist-to-Hip Ratio vs. BMI

BMI compares weight with height and is commonly used as a broad population-level screening measure. WHR compares waist and hip circumferences and provides information about body fat distribution.

These measurements answer different questions. They may be considered together with other information, such as waist circumference, blood pressure, medical history, and laboratory results.

Factors That Can Affect Your WHR

  • Changes in abdominal or hip circumference.
  • Weight changes over time.
  • Pregnancy and postpartum changes.
  • Age-related changes in body composition.
  • Differences in measurement technique.
  • Natural differences in body shape and proportions.

For meaningful tracking, measure under similar conditions and avoid comparing measurements taken using different techniques.

Limitations of Waist-to-Hip Ratio

  • It does not directly measure body fat percentage.
  • It cannot diagnose diabetes, heart disease, or any other condition.
  • Measurement errors can change the result.
  • Reference thresholds may not apply equally to all populations.
  • It should not replace a complete health assessment.
If you are concerned about your measurements or health risks, discuss them with a qualified healthcare professional.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is waist-to-hip ratio?

It is the ratio of waist circumference to hip circumference and is used as one indicator of body fat distribution.

2. How do I calculate waist-to-hip ratio?

Divide your waist measurement by your hip measurement. Both measurements must use the same unit.

3. What is a healthy waist-to-hip ratio?

Common WHO screening thresholds for increased risk are 0.90 or higher for men and 0.85 or higher for women. These are not diagnostic limits.

4. Does WHR diagnose health problems?

No. WHR is a screening measure. A healthcare professional must consider other health information to assess individual risk.

5. Can waist-to-hip ratio change over time?

Yes. Changes in waist or hip circumference can affect the ratio. Consistent measurement technique is important when tracking changes.

6. Is WHR better than BMI?

They measure different things. BMI relates weight to height, while WHR compares waist and hip measurements. Neither provides a complete picture of health on its own.
