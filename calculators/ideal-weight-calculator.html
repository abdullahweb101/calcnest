What Is Ideal Body Weight?

Ideal body weight (IBW) is a traditional term used in several mathematical formulas to estimate a reference body weight based mainly on height and sex. These formulas were developed for particular clinical or research purposes and do not define a universally ideal weight for every person.

People of the same height can have different healthy body compositions, muscle mass, bone structures, and health needs. For that reason, an IBW result should not be treated as a personal goal or a diagnosis.

Remember: There is no single number that determines whether someone is healthy. A qualified healthcare professional can interpret body measurements alongside medical history, body composition, and other relevant information.

Four Ideal Body Weight Formulas Explained

1. Robinson Formula (1983)

Men: 52 kg + 1.9 kg for each inch above 5 feet.
Women: 49 kg + 1.7 kg for each inch above 5 feet.

The Robinson formula is one of several commonly cited historical reference-weight equations.

2. Miller Formula (1983)

Men: 56.2 kg + 1.41 kg for each inch above 5 feet.
Women: 53.1 kg + 1.36 kg for each inch above 5 feet.

Miller's equation uses different constants and generally produces results that differ from other traditional formulas.

3. Devine Formula (1974)

Men: 50 kg + 2.3 kg for each inch above 5 feet.
Women: 45.5 kg + 2.3 kg for each inch above 5 feet.

The Devine formula has historically been used in some clinical calculations, including certain medication-related contexts. It is not a universal healthy-weight standard.

4. Hamwi Formula (1964)

Men: 48 kg + 2.7 kg for each inch above 5 feet.
Women: 45.5 kg + 2.2 kg for each inch above 5 feet.

Hamwi is another traditional equation that provides a height-based reference estimate.

Example: Comparing Formula Results

Consider a hypothetical adult who is 5 feet 9 inches tall (69 inches). The traditional formulas produce different estimates depending on the selected equation category.

For a male example:

  • Robinson: 52 + (1.9 × 9) = 69.1 kg
  • Miller: 56.2 + (1.41 × 9) = 68.89 kg
  • Devine: 50 + (2.3 × 9) = 70.7 kg
  • Hamwi: 48 + (2.7 × 9) = 72.3 kg

The results are not identical because each formula uses different constants. This illustrates why these numbers should be treated as estimates rather than exact targets.

What These Formulas Do Not Measure

Muscle Mass

A person with more muscle may weigh more without that number alone indicating poor health. Height-based formulas do not directly measure muscle mass.

Body Fat Distribution

These equations do not show where body fat is stored or measure body-fat percentage.

Age and Life Stage

The formulas in this calculator do not adjust for age. Growth, aging, pregnancy, and other life stages can require different approaches to assessing weight and health.

Medical History

Health conditions, medications, and personal circumstances may influence what weight-related measurements mean for an individual.

Ideal Weight Calculator FAQs

1. What is ideal body weight?

It is a traditional reference estimate calculated using formulas that mainly consider height and sex. It is not a universal health target.

2. Which ideal weight formula is best?

No single formula is best for everyone. Robinson, Miller, Devine, and Hamwi use different constants and can produce different estimates.

3. Why do the four results differ?

Each formula was developed with different constants and assumptions. Their results are mathematical estimates, not measurements of actual body composition.

4. Does this calculator need my current weight?

No. The four formulas used here rely mainly on height and the selected sex category.

5. Is ideal body weight the same as BMI?

No. BMI is calculated from weight and height. These traditional ideal-weight formulas estimate a reference weight primarily from height and sex.

6. Can athletes use this calculator?

Athletes can view the mathematical estimates, but these formulas do not account for athletic muscle mass or sport-specific requirements.

7. Is this a recommended weight-loss target?

No. The results are not personalized weight-loss goals. A suitable health or nutrition target depends on individual circumstances and professional assessment.

8. Can teenagers use this calculator?

These adult reference formulas are not designed to assess growth or healthy weight in children and teenagers. Age-appropriate professional assessment is important.
