What Is Profit Margin?

Profit margin measures the percentage of revenue that remains as profit after subtracting the cost of a product or service. It helps businesses evaluate pricing and understand how much they earn from sales.

Gross Profit Formula

Gross Profit = Selling Price − Total Cost

Profit Margin Formula

Profit Margin = (Gross Profit ÷ Selling Price) × 100

Markup Formula

Markup = (Gross Profit ÷ Total Cost) × 100

Example Calculation

Suppose a product costs $60 and sells for $100. Gross profit is $40. Profit margin is 40%, while markup is approximately 66.67%. Margin is calculated using selling price; markup is calculated using cost.

Profit Margin vs. Markup

Profit margin and markup are not the same. Profit margin shows profit as a percentage of selling revenue, while markup shows profit as a percentage of cost. Confusing these two figures can lead to incorrect pricing decisions.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. How do I calculate profit margin?

Subtract total cost from selling price, divide the profit by selling price, and multiply by 100.

2. What is a good profit margin?

It depends on the industry, business model, operating expenses and market conditions. There is no single ideal margin for every business.

3. What is the difference between margin and markup?

Margin uses selling price as its base, while markup uses total cost as its base.

4. Can profit margin be negative?

Yes. If total cost is greater than selling price, the business makes a loss and the profit margin becomes negative.

5. Does this calculator include business expenses?

It includes only the total cost you enter. Add applicable expenses to that cost if you want them included in the calculation.
